ImVR: Immersive VR Teleoperation System for General Purpose

Abstract

We present ImVR, an immersive VR-based teleoperation system that places the operator in a fully 3D virtual environment for intuitive and precise robot control. Designed for general-purpose use, ImVR supports a wide range of robot configurations—including single- and dual-arm setups, parallel grippers and multi-fingered dexterous hands—while operating seamlessly in both simulation and the real world. It ensures absolute alignment between the operator’s hand and the robot’s end-effector, offers adaptive viewpoints to enhance situational awareness and precision, and enables easy deployment across diverse robotic platforms. Experiments and user studies highlight ImVR’s versatility, ease of use, and effectiveness in accelerating data collection, making it a powerful tool for large-scale robot learning and real-world manipulation tasks.

Placing the Human in a 3D Virtual World

A key feature of ImVR is that it places the operator directly inside a fully rendered 3D virtual world, rather than behind the 2D video overlays used by prior see-through mixed-reality systems. This gives the operator direct and complete access to the scene's spatial information: stereoscopic 4K video at 60 Hz, real-time head tracking, and natural parallax together restore the depth cues humans rely on for dexterous manipulation. Adaptive viewpoints let the operator smoothly shift between a wide situational overview and a close-up on the end-effector, which is essential for tasks that demand precise alignment or rich contact.

Following the principle of “where your hand is, where the end-effector is,” the system maintains absolute alignment between the operator's hand and the robot's end-effector, so visual feedback stays tightly coupled to hand motion and the operator needs no compensatory motion. Tasks

We demonstrate ImVR across a diverse set of manipulation tasks spanning both simulation and the real world, covering a range of robot platforms (Franka Panda, UFactory XArm6 and XArm7) and end-effectors — parallel grippers, sticks, and multi-fingered dexterous hands including the Allegro, Ability, and Inspire hands.
